# Fairy Tale

## Definition
A fairy tale is a type of short story that typically features folkloric characters such as fairies, goblins, elves, trolls, witches, and giants, and often involves magic or enchantments. These stories are usually set in a fantastical realm and convey moral lessons or cultural values.

## Key Characteristics
- **Folkloric Characters**: Includes a variety of mythical beings like fairies, witches, and giants.
- **Magic and Enchantment**: Often involves magical elements or supernatural events.
- **Moral Lessons**: Typically imparts lessons or warnings, such as the dangers of talking to strangers and the consequences of naivety, as exemplified in "Little Red Riding Hood" (Little Red Riding Hood.pdf).
- **Fantastical Settings**: Takes place in imaginary worlds or distant lands.
- **Cultural Values**: Reflects the traditions and values of the culture from which it originates.

## Context
Fairy tales are a significant part of folklore and literature, serving as a means to pass down cultural values and lessons from generation to generation. They often embody universal themes and morals that resonate across different cultures and time periods. The story of "Little Red Riding Hood" is a classic example, illustrating the dangers of naivety and the importance of caution (Little Red Riding Hood.pdf).

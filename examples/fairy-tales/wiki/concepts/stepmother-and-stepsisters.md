# Stepmother and stepsisters

## Definition
The stepmother and stepsisters are the antagonistic family members of Cinderella in the story. Their vanity and cruelty set them against Cinderella's virtue.

## Key Characteristics
- **Vanity:** They are defined by vanity, which the story presents as a contrast to Cinderella's goodness (Cinderella.pdf).
- **Cruelty:** Their cruelty toward Cinderella makes her virtue stand out (Cinderella.pdf).
- **Humbling:** They are finally humbled when Cinderella forgives them (Cinderella.pdf).
- **Restoration through forgiveness:** Cinderella secures them places in the palace after forgiving them (Cinderella.pdf).

## Context
Within the story, the stepmother and stepsisters function mainly as foils to Cinderella. Their vanity and cruelty throw her virtue into relief (Cinderella.pdf). Their arc resolves not through punishment but through Cinderella's forgiveness, which humbles them and results in her securing them places in the palace (Cinderella.pdf).

### Comparison with the stepmother in Snow White
The stepmother figure appears in other tales with a different motive. In *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs*, the stepmother is a Queen who is vain and wicked, and she plots to kill her stepdaughter out of envy of her beauty rather than from family favoritism (Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs.pdf). Both figures are defined by vanity, but the Snow White stepmother's hostility is driven by rivalry over beauty and is carried to murderous intent, while the Cinderella stepfamily's role is to serve as foils to the heroine's virtue and is resolved through forgiveness (Cinderella.pdf; Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs.pdf).
